# Thay Earnings Calendar Launch

**For:** Thay
**Requested:** Plan a launch campaign for Thay's new earnings calendar feature, with ideas across copy, social, and growth
**Specialists involved:** content-strategist, copywriter, social-media-manager, growth-marketer

## Summary

All four pieces build from one strategy brief and stay coherent: instead of pitching "a calendar" (something every trading app already has and nobody opens), the whole campaign frames this as **"stop checking, start getting told"** — Thay already knows what you hold, so it surfaces your earnings dates unprompted, inside the chat you're already in. The LINE broadcast (primary vehicle, retention-focused) and the social post/ideas (secondary, reaches people still juggling a broker app + spreadsheet) share the same before/after "3 apps vs. one chat" motif rather than each piece reinventing the angle. Growth recommends organic-only for this launch, with paid social scoped as a contingent follow-on once organic engagement validates the angle — not a day-one spend.

## Flags for review

Brand review (`marketing:brand-review`) was run over the full package before assembly. Two items need a human decision before anything here goes out:

1. **Confirm with product: is the earnings calendar push-only, or can users query it on demand?** The LINE broadcast's closing CTA assumes users can ask Thay a live question ("หุ้นที่ถืออยู่ ตัวไหนยังไม่ประกาศผลบ้าง") and get an answer today, even before the proactive push starts firing. If the feature is push-only at launch, cut that closing paragraph before sending — as written it could set an expectation the feature doesn't meet.
2. **Time-sensitive copy — do not reuse past ~late August 2026.** The LINE broadcast and one tweet both lean on "Q2 earnings season is still closing out, some of your names haven't reported yet" — accurate now, stale within weeks. The strategy brief flags a natural second beat around the Q3 kickoff (mid-October) as a separate future push, not a reuse of this copy.

Lower-severity, already handled by the authors: the LINE broadcast's "so you won't find out later that a stock you hold already reported" line stays on the informational side of the compliance guardrail (you'll know the date, not "you'll profit") but is the single line doing the most work against it — worth one more human read. No "sure win," guaranteed-return, or outcome-promising language appears anywhere in the package; pillar 4 (consensus estimates / "why this print matters") was consistently and correctly omitted across every piece since it's unconfirmed with product.
